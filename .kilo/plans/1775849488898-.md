## Research Findings (Industry Best Practices)
Analysis of 2026 AI video prompting guides reveals consistent patterns for cinematic quality:

### 1. 5-Layer Framework (Universal)
- **Layer 1: Camera Language** - Shot types, movement verbs, lens specifications (35mm, 85mm, shallow DOF)
- **Layer 2: Lighting & Atmosphere** - Motivated sources, color temperature, volumetric effects
- **Layer 3: Action & Motion** - Physical plausibility, pacing control, emotional arcs
- **Layer 4: Texture & Material** - Surface details, material properties, environmental continuity
- **Layer 5: Reference Style** - Film stock emulation, genre signals, technical specs

### 2. Advanced Camera Techniques
- **Movement**: dolly in/out, tracking shots, crane shots, whip pans, steadicam
- **Lens Language**: focal length feel (24mm wide, 85mm compression), depth of field control
- **Composition**: rule of thirds, leading lines, negative space, aspect ratio adaptation
- **Perspective**: low-angle, bird's eye, Dutch angles, over-the-shoulder

### 3. Professional Lighting Design
- **Motivated Sources**: practical lamps, window light, neon signs, car headlights
- **Technical Setup**: three-point lighting, rim lighting, chiaroscuro contrast
- **Atmospheric Effects**: volumetric god rays, haze, light shafts, lens flares
- **Color Temperature**: precise Kelvin values, white balance control

### 4. Production Quality Enhancements
- **Film Stock Emulation**: ARRI Alexa look, 35mm grain, anamorphic flares
- **Color Grading**: teal & orange, vintage film, high contrast presets
- **Motion Control**: speed ramps, overcranked slow motion, beat-synced pacing
- **Audio Integration**: room tone, spatial audio, Foley enhancement

## Risks and Mitigation
### Technical Risks
- **Prompt Length Explosion**: Cinematic enhancements could exceed token limits
  - *Mitigation*: Implement smart truncation and priority-based inclusion
- **Model Compatibility**: Not all AI models support advanced cinematic vocabulary
  - *Mitigation*: Fallback to simpler language for unsupported models
- **Performance Impact**: Enhanced prompts may increase generation time
  - *Mitigation*: Optional enhancement system with performance monitoring

### Quality Risks
- **Over-Enhancement**: Too much cinematic language could confuse models
  - *Mitigation*: Quality scoring system prevents prompt bloat
- **Inconsistent Results**: Different models interpret cinematic terms differently
  - *Mitigation*: Model-specific enhancement mappings and testing
- **User Overwhelm**: Too many cinematic options could complicate interface
  - *Mitigation*: Progressive disclosure with basic/advanced modes

### Business Risks
- **Backward Compatibility**: Existing users expect current quality levels
  - *Mitigation*: Enhancements are optional, existing templates unchanged
- **Learning Curve**: Users may struggle with cinematic terminology
  - *Mitigation*: Presets, tooltips, and guided workflows
- **Performance Cost**: Enhanced prompts may increase API costs
  - *Mitigation*: Quality-scored enhancements with cost-benefit analysis

## Cinematic + Human-Realism Quality Guardrails (Priority)
Goal: ensure prompts produce the right video type with strong cinematic realism, especially for subway videos.

### 1) Type-Locking Rules
- For muapi outputs, enforce explicit `video_type` intent in compiled prompt instructions.
- Add hard constraints per mode (subway/street/studio/wisdom/motivational) so planning cannot drift into mismatched visual grammar.
- Validate selected strategy against chosen mode before enabling generate.

### 2) Cinematic Baseline Rules (All Modes)
- Require camera language in prompt (framing, lens feel, motion style, depth cues).
- Require physically plausible lighting directions and mood consistency.
- Require environmental continuity (location details, texture, ambient movement, no synthetic artifacts).
- Explicitly forbid common AI artifacts (plastic skin, warped hands, floating props, unreadable signage, uncanny faces).

### 3) Human-Realism Rules (All Modes)
- Enforce natural micro-expressions, breathing, conversational pacing, and real-world body mechanics.
- Require authentic wardrobe/material descriptions and realistic skin/hair texture cues.
- Disallow over-stylized/CGI/illustrative aesthetics unless explicitly requested.

### 4) Subway-Specific Realism Hardening (Critical)
- Keep existing subway identity constraints from prompt engine (subway environment + interviewer identity/card-mic rules).
- Add muapi planning checks that always include:
  - real transit environment anchors (MTA/line/city cues as appropriate)
  - handheld documentary motion language
  - commuter background behavior and ambient context
  - clear mic-card visibility and prohibition of traditional mics
- Preserve strict subway negatives already in engine and expand with realism negatives (waxy skin, over-clean set, fake extras).

### 5) Prompt QA Gate Before Generation
- Add preflight `PromptQualityCheck` for muapi-generated prompts:
  - `type_match_score`
  - `cinematic_score`
  - `human_realism_score`
  - `subway_authenticity_score` (only for subway mode)
- If score below threshold, auto-rewrite once with stronger constraints before calling generation.
- Surface a concise warning if rewrite still below threshold and block generation only on severe type mismatch.
